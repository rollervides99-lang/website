Project highlights

Modeling and validation at the center.

Dakota uses ensemble learning and systematic model evaluation to generate robust equity signals with measurable out-of-sample performance.

XGBoost architecture

My XGBoost model consists of 300 sequential regression trees. Each tree has a maximum depth of four and attempts to correct the prediction errors remaining from the previous trees. The learning rate limits each tree to 5% of its proposed adjustment. Each tree is trained on 90% of the observations and 80% of the features, which adds randomness and reduces overfitting. L2 regularization (lambda) is set to 1.5 to shrink extreme leaf predictions. The model minimizes squared error and produces a predicted next-quarter log return; performance is evaluated with RMSE.

LightGBM robustness

LightGBM follows a comparable boosting approach with feature- and sample-level randomness (90% of observations, 80% of features per tree). For LightGBM we optimize Huber loss to reduce sensitivity to large residuals and improve robustness in heavy-tailed return distributions. Regularization and early stopping are applied to control complexity and maintain stable out-of-sample behavior.

Meta learner

The meta learner ingests out-of-fold predictions from the base learners and is trained to minimize RMSE. By weighting and combining XGBoost and LightGBM outputs, the meta layer produces a consolidated signal with improved predictive accuracy for next-quarter log returns.

OOF validation

Out-of-fold (OOF) validation is applied to the SGB and LGB base learners only. OOF ensures that each observation is evaluated on predictions from models that did not see that observation during training, providing an unbiased estimate of generalization and reducing the risk of overfit when stacking models.
